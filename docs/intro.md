# Getting started

Deal Desk turns a conversation into a proposal your customer can sign. This page takes you from a
new account to a signed, sealed document.

## 1. Set up your letterhead once

Open **Company profile** and fill in your company name, logo, website, brand colour, and the person
who signs on your behalf. Everything you send from then on carries it — the proposal and the email
that delivers it.

Add your **signatory email** too. Signing requests show your name and come back to that address
when the customer replies.

## 2. Draft from what you already have

Paste call notes, a transcript, or an email thread into **Draft from notes** and press
**Draft with AI**. On a phone you can press the microphone and describe the job out loud instead.

What comes back is a complete draft: scope, line items, discounts, terms, both signature blocks.
Every field is editable.

:::tip
The messier the input the better it works. Half-sentences, crossed-out numbers and "roughly 18k for
phase one" are exactly what it expects.
:::

## 3. Check the required fields

Before you can export, Deal Desk checks that the customer name, the customer signatory and their
email, the scope overview and your own signatory are all present. A missing field is highlighted
and the section it lives in is opened for you.

## 4. Get it signed

**In person** — tap either signature line. The pad fills the screen. The signer types their name,
accepts the electronic signature consent, and signs with a finger, stylus or mouse.

**Remotely** — press **Request remote signature**. The customer gets a single-use link that expires
in 72 hours, and signs on their own device. If email is configured they also receive a one-time
code.

## 5. Seal it

Press **Seal & download**. You get the final PDF with a **Certificate of Completion** on its own
page recording who signed, when, from where and how they were identified — plus a verification id.

Anyone holding the file can check it at `dealdesk.studio/verify/<id>`. The page re-computes the
file's SHA-256 in their browser and compares it with the sealed value. Any change breaks the match.
